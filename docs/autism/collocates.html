How this was made

Each variant was searched with the Ngram Viewer's wildcards, once per decade: variant * and * variant for any word, *_NOUN and *_ADJ for words tagged as nouns or adjectives. The viewer returns its top 10 completions for the period searched, so every decade has its own top 10.

Wildcard searches are case-sensitive, so every variant was searched in lower case and with a capital first letter (“autism” and “Autism”, “asperger” and “Asperger”). The completions were lower-cased and added up, then ranked again. A word that is in one casing's top 10 but not the other's counts only the first, so shares are slight underestimates.

The share is the word's frequency divided by the variant's own frequency in the same decade. Part-of-speech tags are assigned automatically by Google and are sometimes wrong, especially in older books, and pieces of hyphenated words split across lines (“chil-”) are dropped. The 2010s column covers 2010 to 2022. Decades in which a variant does not appear at all are left out, and the two five-word variants (“childhood onset pervasive developmental disorder(s)”) could not be searched with a wildcard.

Data: Google Books Ngram Viewer, English corpus (2019 edition), 1800–2022, no smoothing.
