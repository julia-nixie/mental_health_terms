How to read this

Source: Google Books Ngram Viewer, English corpus, 1800–2022. A value is the share of all phrases of that length in the year's books, shown per million.

Each term adds together its spelling variants, for example "attention deficit hyperactivity disorder", "attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder" and "ADHD", and the plural forms. Abbreviations were matched in capitals only. Abbreviations that are also everyday words or abbreviations (ED, AN, SAD, DID, ODD, BED, HAND, TS, BN) were left out, because they mostly counted something else. Variants longer than five words were shortened to their first five, and shortened forms shared by several terms were left out.

A category adds its own term and every term under it. Phrases that contain other phrases are counted for both, so a category total can be somewhat higher than the number of distinct books that mention it. Frequencies show how often words are used in print, not how common a condition is. Some words, such as "mania", also had everyday meanings long before they were diagnoses.
